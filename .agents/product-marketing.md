# Voice and audience — Eunice & Warren

Context for the `copy-editing` skill and anyone editing site copy. The site is a
wedding invitation, not a product: "conversion" here means a guest RSVPs, knows
where to be, and feels welcome.

## What the site is

- The wedding of Eunice and Warren: Thursday 14 January 2027, Boracay,
  Philippines. All times are Philippine Time (GMT+8).
- One page: invitation, schedule, attire, getting there and where to stay,
  things to do on the island, entourage, FAQ, RSVP, a playlist with song requests,
  and With Love (guests' photo-and-message polaroids).
- RSVP by name lookup against the guest list; each invitation lists the seats saved
  for that household. The deadline is in December 2026.

## Audience

About 100 invited guests: family and close friends, many travelling from Manila and
abroad, a range of ages. Some read on a phone in transit. They need dates, places,
what to wear and what to do next, without hunting.

## Voice

- Warm, personal and light. The couple speaks as "we" to "you".
- Short sentences. Plain words. Practical first, sentiment second.
- A little playful where it fits ("Pack light. Bring your dancing shoes."), never
  cute at the expense of clarity.
- Filipino words the guests use are welcome as they are (ninong, ninang, barong,
  Filipiniana, salamat), not translated or italicised.
- Gracious about limits: say what is and is not possible kindly and directly
  ("Your invitation lists everyone we've saved a seat for.").

## Rules

- **No em dashes in site copy.** Use a full stop, a comma, a colon or "and".
  The user audited them out of the pre-wedding section; the rest of the site still
  has a few (Hero date line, Entourage intro, Stay intro, two RSVP prompts) to
  clear when that copy is next touched.
- Dates in prose read "14 January 2027" or "Thu, Jan 14, 2027"; display lines may
  use the stamp style `01 · 14 · 2027`.
- Placeholders such as `[December date]` stay until the user gives the value; never
  invent a date, price, address or name.
- Guest names in examples are fictional. Never put a real guest's name in copy,
  fixtures or tests.
